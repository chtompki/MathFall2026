    \noindent\textbf{1.} Let $G$ be an $n$-vertex simple planar graph with girth $k$.
    Prove that $G$ has at most $(n-2)\frac{k}{k-2}$ edges.
    Use this to prove that the Petersen graph is nonplanar.
    (Hint: Mimic the proof of Theorem 6.1.23.
    What is the smallest possible length of a face?)

    \begin{proof}
        If $G$ is disconnected, draw its components separately and join them
        by bridges to obtain a connected simple plane graph $H$ on the same
        $n$ vertices. If $G$ is connected, take $H$ to be a plane drawing of
        $G$. The added bridges create no cycles, so $H$ still has girth $k$.

        Every face of $H$ has boundary length at least $k$, counting edge
        occurrences with multiplicity. To see this, contract all bridges.
        Contracting a bridge preserves the faces and the cycles, and removes
        two occurrences of that bridge from its incident face boundary.
        The resulting connected plane graph has a cycle because the girth
        is finite. Each face therefore has a nonempty boundary walk, and
        each edge on that walk occurs only once because every edge is
        incident with two distinct faces. Every nonempty closed trail
        contains a cycle, whose length is at least $k$.

        Write $e=e(H)$ and let $f$ be the number of its faces. Counting the
        two sides of every edge gives $kf\le 2e$. Euler's formula yields
        \[
            2=n-e+f\le n-e+\frac{2e}{k},
            \qquad
            e(G)\le e\le (n-2)\frac{k}{k-2}.
        \]

        Represent the Petersen graph by the two-element subsets of
        $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, with adjacency defined by disjointness. There are
        ten vertices, each of degree three, so there are fifteen edges.
        Three pairwise disjoint two-element sets would require six
        elements, so there is no triangle. Two distinct vertices have
        at most one common neighbor, since the complement of their union
        has at most two elements. A $4$-cycle would give its opposite
        vertices two common neighbors. The sequence
        $12,34,15,23,45,12$, where $ij$ denotes $\{i,j\}$, is a $5$-cycle.
        Hence the girth is five. If the Petersen graph were planar, the
        bound just proved would give $15\le 8\cdot 5/3=40/3$, a contradiction.
    \end{proof}

    \noindent\textbf{2.} Prove that the complement of a simple planar graph with at least
    11 vertices is nonplanar.
    (Hint: If $G$ and $\overline{G}$ are both planar, apply Theorem 6.1.23 to each of them.)

    \begin{proof}
        Suppose both $G$ and $\overline G$ are planar. Each pair of
        vertices contributes exactly one edge to the two graphs together.
        Applying Theorem~6.1.23 to each graph gives
        \[
            \binom n2=e(G)+e(\overline G)\le 6n-12.
        \]
        But for $n\ge 11$ we have
        \[
            \binom n2-(6n-12)
            =\frac{(n-11)(n-2)+2}{2}>0.
        \]
        This contradiction shows that $\overline G$ is nonplanar.
    \end{proof}

    \noindent\textbf{3.} Let $S$ be a set of $n$ points in the plane such that for all $x,y\in S$,
    the distance in the plane between $x$ and $y$ is at least 1.
    Prove that there are at most $3n-6$ pairs $u,v\in S$ such that the distance in the plane
    between $u$ and $v$ is exactly 1.
    (Hint: Join each such pair $u,v$ with a line segment.
    Show that no two of the segments cross, then use Theorem 6.1.23.)

    \begin{proof}
        Assume $n\ge 3$, as required by Theorem~6.1.23, and interpret the
        distance condition for distinct points. For $n=1$ the asserted
        bound is negative, and for $n=2$ a unit-distance pair exceeds
        the bound of zero.

        Draw a graph on $S$ by joining each unit-distance pair by its
        straight segment. No vertex can lie in the interior of an edge,
        since it would be less than one unit from an endpoint.
        Suppose edges $ab$ and $cd$ with distinct endpoints meet at an
        interior point $p$. Choose the labels so that $a$ and $c$ are
        the endpoints closest to $p$ on their respective edges. Then
        $|a-p|\le 1/2$ and $|c-p|\le 1/2$. If the segments lie on
        different lines, the strict triangle inequality gives
        \[
            |a-c|<|a-p|+|p-c|\le 1,
        \]
        a contradiction. If the segments are collinear and overlap,
        one has an endpoint in the interior of the other, which was
        already ruled out. Distinct edges with a common endpoint can
        meet only there, since any further intersection would force
        the two unit segments to coincide.

        Thus, the drawing is planar. Theorem~6.1.23 bounds its number
        of edges, and hence the number of unit-distance pairs, by $3n-6$.
    \end{proof}
